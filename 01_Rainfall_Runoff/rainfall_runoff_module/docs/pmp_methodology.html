PMP Methodology — how the plugin calculates each component

This page documents every arithmetic step the plugin performs for the GSDM, GTSMR and GSAM methods, and links every step to the corresponding section of the Bureau of Meteorology's guidebooks. If a plugin result disagrees with an external estimate, this is where to check which rule is being applied.

1. Overview of PMP methods

The Bureau of Meteorology's Australian PMP methods each cover a different duration and terrain range:

MethodDurationsAreal limitAdjustments applied
GSDM — Generalised Short-Duration Method 0.25 – 6 hr Up to 1 000 km² terrain-weighted depth, Elevation Adjustment Factor (EAF), Moisture Adjustment Factor (MAF)
GTSMR — Generalised Tropical Storm Method Revised 24 – 120 hr 1 – 150 000 km² TAF, DAF, and MAF (per season: summer + winter)
GSAM — Generalised Southeast Australia Method 24 – 96 hr 1 – 100 000 km² TAF and MAF only (no DAF). Summer + autumn.

Every method returns depth as a function of duration. Each duration's final PMP is rounded to the nearest 10 mm; the controlling duration is picked on the unrounded depths so a rounding tie can never move the controlling duration. Code: pmp_toolbox.core.depth.round_pmp.

2. PMP zone lookup

Every catchment centroid is intersected with the Bureau's own zone polygons (from the GTSMR CD's pmp_zones/pmp_zones.shp). The zone determines which method(s) apply and which zone code is passed to the DDA table.

Reference: GTSMR guidebook (2003), Figure 1 and §2.1. GSAM guidebook (2006), Figure 1. The plugin ships the same polygons from the GTSMR CD 2005 — see data/zones_all.json.

Ray-casting point-in-polygon runs against a simplified copy of the rings (0.9 km simplification, islands under about 40 km² dropped) so a small island can miss the zone. The transition zones make both methods applicable at once; the plugin now flags both as usable and seeds the GSAM Coastal/Autumn zones so you can compare the two answers as the guidebook expects. Code: pmp_toolbox.core.zones.lookup.

3. Depth–Duration–Area (DDA) lookup

Every method reads its raw depth from a tabulated Depth-Duration-Area table shipped in data/ as CSV. Interpolation is log-linear in area and linear in duration:

D(area, duration) = D(area_lo, duration_lo)
    + (D(area_hi, duration_lo) - D(area_lo, duration_lo)) *
      (log(area) - log(area_lo)) / (log(area_hi) - log(area_lo))
    ... same for area_hi, then interpolate between duration_lo, duration_hi.
Reference:
  • GSDM Figure 4 curves (digitised from GSDM_Depth_Area_Curves_Digitise.xlsx) drive DDACurves.
  • GTSMR tables and GSAM tables come straight from the Bureau CDs (depth_area_data/*.xls) and drive AreaDepthTable.
Code: pmp_toolbox.core.dda.

4. Catchments smaller than 1 km²

The GTSMR / GSAM depth-area tables tabulate down to 1 km². When your catchment is smaller, the plugin gives you a choice on the GTSMR/GSAM inputs tab:

  • Default (checkbox off) — clamp at 1 km². The 1 km² depth is used for any smaller catchment. This matches the Bureau's own Toolbox website and follows §2.2 of the guidebooks, which describes the areal limit.
  • Extrapolate below 1 km² (checkbox on). Log-linearly extrapolate off the 1 km² and 1.5 km² rows. This matches spreadsheet templates that carry a smaller catchment through without clamping (e.g. PSM's PMP_Estimation template). Depths will be slightly higher because the log-area curves rise as area shrinks.

Either way, the plugin adds a warning to the result table that says which behaviour applied. Code: pmp_toolbox.core.dda.AreaDepthTable.depth.

5. Moisture Adjustment Factor (MAF)

MAF scales the raw depth from a standard storm to the catchment's climate:

MAF = EPW_catchment / EPW_standard

where the standard values are prescribed by the guidebook:

Method / seasonEPW standardGuidebook reference
GTSMR summer120.0 mmGTSMR §2.3.1
GTSMR winter82.3 mmGTSMR §2.3.1
GSAM summer80.8 mmGSAM §2.3
GSAM autumn71.0 mmGSAM §2.3

EPW_catchment is either sampled at the catchment centroid or averaged over the polygon (see §8).

Common trap: the GSAM autumn EPW is not the GTSMR winter EPW. Some workbooks re-use the winter grid; the plugin reads GSAM's own epw_autumn.asc from the Oct 2006 CD. Substituting the winter value into GSAM autumn will produce PMPs that are roughly 10–15% too low along the NSW coast.

6. Topographic Adjustment Factor (TAF)

TAF is a dimensionless ratio (typically 0.95 – 1.5) that lifts or suppresses the design storm to account for orographic effects. It comes straight off a gridded surface:

  • GTSMR uses ifdr005m.asc from the GTSMR CD.
  • GSAM uses gsam_taf.asc from the GSAM CD. This grid only covers Australia east of about 118 °E — outside that footprint the plugin falls back to the GTSMR TAF.

Both grids are quantised and packed into data/gtsmr_grids.npz at 0.05° resolution (from 0.005° native). Resampling error measured against the full-resolution grids: < 0.0005 for TAF, < 0.010 mm for EPW.

Reference: GTSMR §2.3.2, GSAM §2.3. Code: pmp_toolbox.core.grids.lookup and pmp_toolbox.core.grids.average_at_points.

7. Decay Amplitude Factor (DAF) — GTSMR only

The DAF captures how the design storm's rainfall depth decays with duration relative to a reference storm; the number is applied to the GTSMR depths only. It comes from the same 0.005° grid packed at 0.05° into daf.

Reference: GTSMR §2.3.3. GSAM has no DAF.

8. Centroid vs catchment-averaged sampling

The guidebooks (§2.3 in both) prescribe that grid-derived factors are averaged over the catchment outline. In practice most worked spreadsheets sample at the centroid because it is faster and reproduces the guidebook worked examples to within 0.03%. The plugin now does the guidebook version: it walks every 0.05° cell centre whose point-in-polygon falls inside the catchment and averages those cells per grid.

How the average is computed, step by step

  1. Reproject the catchment polygon to WGS84 (EPSG:4326).
  2. Snap the polygon's bounding box to the packed grid's index range.
  3. For every grid cell centre in that range, run QgsGeometryEngine.intersects against the polygon.
  4. Keep the (lat, lon) pairs that landed inside; discard the rest.
  5. For every grid (EPW summer, EPW winter, TAF, DAF, GSAM autumn, GSAM TAF), sample the value at each kept cell and average. NODATA cells are skipped per grid, so a coastal fringe still produces sensible numbers.

If the catchment is smaller than one 0.05° cell (i.e. no cell centre lands inside), the plugin falls back to the centroid value. This guarantees a sub-30 km² catchment always gets an answer. Code: qgis_bridge.catchment_grid_points + grids.average_at_points.

For sub-1 km² catchments the two methods give the same number because one cell dominates. For large catchments straddling a steep TAF gradient (coastal escarpment) the area-averaged number can differ from the centroid by a few percent — that difference is the signal §2.3 was written to capture.

9. GSAM autumn: a separate EPW grid

Historically, spreadsheet workflows re-used the GTSMR winter EPW grid (gtsmrwin005pwm.asc) for GSAM autumn. Winter and autumn are different meteorological seasons, and at NSW coastal sites this substitution understates the autumn EPW by around 10 mm. The plugin ships the GSAM CD's own epw_autumn.asc as gsam_epw_autumn and reads from it in the GSAM autumn seed. This is the largest single error corrected in the switch from workbook to plugin.

10. GSDM terrain weighting and EAF

GSDM has two DDA curves per duration (Smooth and Rough). The catchment's terrain fraction weights them:

D = S * D_smooth + R * D_rough        (S + R = 1)
Reference: GSDM §4.2.

The Elevation Adjustment Factor (EAF) is 1.0 below 1500 m and rises linearly with mean catchment elevation above that.

Reference: GSDM §4.3. Code: pmp_toolbox.core.gsdm.

Finally, GSDM depths are scaled by MAF (from Figure 3) — not the gridded EPW ratio that GTSMR / GSAM use. In the plugin the MAF value is entered directly on the GSDM tab.

  • GSAM CD Oct 2006 — ships epw_annual.asc, epw_autumn.asc, and gsam_taf.asc.

Source code the calculations live in (all under the pmp_toolbox package):

  • core/depth.py — the per-method arithmetic (calc_gsdm, calc_gtsmr, calc_gsam) and the shared _long_duration that applies MAF and TAF / DAF.
  • core/dda.py — log-in-area / linear-in-duration interpolation over the DDA tables. Also the sub-1 km² clamp / extrapolate switch.
  • core/grids.py — lookup for centroid sampling, average_at_points for the area-averaged path.
  • core/zones.py — PMP application zone lookup and transition-zone handling.
  • qgis_bridge.py — catchment_grid_points enumerates the cells inside the catchment polygon for the area-averaged path.
